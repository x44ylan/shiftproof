# Design evidence

## Audience and hypothesis

The intended user is the person coordinating a volunteer rota for a small event. The hypothesis is that a changed schedule is easier to review when the organiser can see its consequences and give each volunteer a short personal notice. This has not been tested with real volunteers. The fictional example demonstrates implementation, not measured impact.

## Primary guidance

- NCVO, *Keeping volunteers*: https://www.ncvo.org.uk/help-and-guidance/involving-volunteers/supporting-and-managing-volunteers/keeping-volunteers/ — recommends providing the information volunteers need, keeping them updated in a suitable way, communicating important changes promptly, and removing inaccessible processes. This informed individual before/after cards and printable output.
- NCVO, *Supporting volunteers*: https://www.ncvo.org.uk/help-and-guidance/involving-volunteers/supporting-and-managing-volunteers/supporting-volunteers/ — emphasises individual needs, clear support and supervision, and logging/debriefing systems. This informed explicit organiser review and the distinction between a free slot in a rota and a person's consent.

These pages were checked October 4, 2026 UTC. They do not establish a rate of missed shifts, financial savings or effectiveness of this app.

## Contest fit

Devpost's winner interviews informed the scope and presentation:

- https://info.devpost.com/blog/user-story-ansh — a focused prototype with a few essential features and a strong interface. Applied to compare, repair and share.
- https://info.devpost.com/blog/user-story-nathan — graphical interactions can distinguish an entry from common chatbot approaches. Applied to before/after rota bars and personal change cards. The interview links the award-winning Solace project: https://devpost.com/software/solace-p216ij
- https://info.devpost.com/blog/user-story-tristan — reserve substantial time for the demo and submission. Applied to repeatable E2E evidence and a recorded product walkthrough.

These lessons informed implementation decisions; they do not establish global novelty or predict an award.

LovHack Season 3's published judging weights are execution/functionality 35%, problem/impact 25%, innovation/creativity 20%, and presentation/user experience 20%. Shiftproof therefore concentrates on one complete, visible workflow rather than a broad feature list. AI-assisted development is disclosed. Public rules: https://lovhack-season-3.devpost.com/rules

Graphiques accepts original research-backed concepts and optional demonstrations. The proposed service design is a small-event review process with a runnable interaction prototype. Public rules: https://graphiques-challenge.devpost.com/rules

HACK47 OFFGRID accepts original projects and AI development tools, judging originality, impact, execution, product thinking, technical depth and potential. Public rules: https://hack47-offgrid.devpost.com/rules

## Next validation

Before claiming practical benefit, ask real organisers to review a schedule change with their current tools and with Shiftproof. Record overlooked issues, comprehension of individual notices, accessibility problems and consent practices. This is proposed research; no such study has been conducted.
